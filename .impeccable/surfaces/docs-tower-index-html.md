# Surface brief: the Timekeepers' Tower treatment (docs/tower/index.html)

## Scope and mode

An alternate treatment of the Goodput Festival of Code landing page, reached from the Treatment dropdown in every header. Same event, same facts, a different visual world. Mode: **Persuade**.

## Audience, job, action

- Audience: AI engineers and builders first; technical leaders and buyers second.
- Job: decide whether this is a real technical event worth two days in San Francisco.
- Action: **Request an invitation**. The URL is one shared setting (`docs/assets/js/config.js`). While it is empty the closing pit board says entries open shortly and makes "Save the date" its working action.
- Proof and content: the user's programme facts only. No record figure, time, agenda slot or talk title is invented: anything undecided is an empty hook on the board, marked "to be posted".
- Constraints: static files in `docs/tower/`; shared `config.js` and `treatments.js`; no logos or photographs; portraits are original drawings.

## Direction contract

**THESIS.** The festival is announced the way a race is scored: a trackside scoreboard tower whose painted boards and hung number tiles carry every fact, and whose record board still has empty hooks. It refuses the software dashboard (small tiles, many metrics, panels) and the tech-conference template alike: tiles are few, man-high and hung by hand.

**OWN-WORLD.** Racing-green gloss boards bolted to a black timber frame whose two posts show at the sides; plank seams and bolt heads are drawn. Cream enamel tiles with black numerals hang on hooks that show through their two holes, a little out of true and unevenly spaced. A chrome-yellow pit board with black block lettering and a painted keyline. Red only on cream, or as the warning board. Type follows the material: what is painted is set in signwriter's block capitals (names, titles) or a tall gothic (numerals, column heads, painted sentences); what is typed or pencilled is always on paper, either the ruled ledger sheet (every line of type on a 32px rule) or a plain slip pinned to a board. One module sizes every tile, gap, board and painted letter. An unposted place is a bare pair of hooks; full recesses are kept for the record board. Flat colour and one coarse hatch only.

**STORY.** At a glance: a two-day festival of AI inference, 27 and 28 April 2027, Palace of Fine Arts, San Francisco, scored like a race meeting. The visitor comes to believe the main event is a real attempt (the record board is waiting for its figure) and that the entry list is worth the trip. They act on the pit board: request an invitation.

**FIRST VIEWPORT.** A tower of three green boards between two posts, filling the width (it narrows to keep its proportions when the window is short). Top board: GOODPUT in inline signwriter's capitals at the left, "Festival of Code" at the right edge. Middle board: 27 & 28 in man-high cream tiles with a scorer on a ladder hanging the last one; "April 2027" and the venue lines painted in a right column that shares the pit board's right edge. Bottom board: the offer typed on a pinned slip, "World record · tokens per second" over five empty recesses marked "to be posted", and the yellow pit board reading REQUEST AN INVITATION. The running fascia above carries the name, the Treatment dropdown and a small pit board with when, where and the action.

**FORM.** The Timekeepers' Tower. seed: none (pinned). The user's words: "Let's do the other two treatments as well.", naming the cards presented on the decision page, of which this is "The Timekeepers' Tower". Signature interaction: hang your forecast. The visitor types a tokens-per-second forecast on a pinned slip and it is hung tile by tile on the record board's second row, under the official row whose hooks stay empty; the scorer stands on his ladder beneath that row, reaching up to it. Motion grammar: tiles drop onto their hooks and settle with one small swing; nothing loops.

**FINISH.** unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
